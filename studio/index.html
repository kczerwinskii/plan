Desktop app for short-form video

Plan, publish and review short videos in one place.

Kuba Studio is a Windows desktop app made by Jakub (Kuba) Czerwiński, an online personal trainer. He records short training videos for Instagram and uses Studio to schedule and publish each finished video to his own Instagram, Facebook, YouTube and TikTok accounts, and to see how his own Instagram reels performed.

Studio is a private tool. It runs locally on its owner's computer, has no sign-up and no user accounts, and is not sold or distributed. This website describes what the app does, which platform APIs it uses and how it handles data.

Who it is for

Built around one trainer's publishing routine

Kuba coaches his clients fully online, and Instagram (@kubaczerwinskii) is his main channel. Every video is made for Instagram first. Facebook, YouTube and TikTok are extra places where the same finished video gets published, so Studio analyses Instagram only and simply publishes to the other platforms.

The app is shaped for a creator who works alone: one video at a time, little data on screen at once, and a clear status for every platform after each post.

What it does

Features

Analysis

Review your own Instagram reels

Studio reads the statistics of the owner's own reels from the Instagram API: views, reach, average watch time, likes, comments, saves, shares and skip rate. It compares each reel's retention with his own median, taking the video's length into account. Instead of grading a reel as good or bad, it points to where to look: the hook, the middle or the ending.

Publishing

Plan and publish to four platforms

Add a finished video, write the caption, pick a cover frame and choose the date and the platforms: Instagram, Facebook, YouTube and TikTok. A weekly calendar shows what is scheduled. Each platform keeps its own settings, such as the YouTube title and privacy, or the TikTok privacy, interaction and disclosure options.

Accounts

See your own accounts in one view

Follower and subscriber counts of the owner's own Instagram account, Facebook Page and YouTube channel, shown side by side. For TikTok it shows only the name and profile picture of the connected account.

How it works

From finished video to published post

  1. Connect the accounts once

    In Settings, the owner connects each of his own accounts once: Instagram and Facebook with a Meta access token for his own Meta app, YouTube with Google OAuth and TikTok with TikTok Login Kit. Access tokens are saved only on his computer.

  2. Add a video

    He picks a finished MP4 or MOV file from his disk. Studio reads its length and prepares cover frame options locally, on his computer.

  3. Prepare the post

    He writes the caption, picks the cover, sets a date or leaves the video unscheduled, and ticks the platforms. For YouTube and TikTok he fills in that platform's own settings before the video can be sent.

  4. Publish

    Nothing is posted until he clicks Publish now, or until the date and time he set himself (scheduling works while Studio is open). The video file is uploaded straight from his computer to each platform's official API.

  5. Check the result

    Studio shows the status for every platform separately, with a link where the platform returns one. Published Instagram reels then appear in Analysis.

Platforms and APIs

Where Studio publishes

Instagram

Instagram Graph API (Meta). Publishes reels to the owner's professional account and reads the statistics of his own reels.

Facebook

Graph API (Meta). Publishes the same reel to the owner's own Facebook Page.

YouTube

YouTube Data API. Uploads the video to the owner's own channel. How Studio uses YouTube

TikTok

Login Kit and Content Posting API with Direct Post. Posts the video to the owner's own profile with the settings he chooses. How Studio uses TikTok
